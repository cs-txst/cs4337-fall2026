What Moved

Subtracting one frame from the next finds motion and finds noise at the same time. One Gaussian blur, applied before the subtraction, separates the two. Here is the knob that decides.

The camera

Real sensors add a little noise to every pixel of every frame, independently. Two frames of a completely static scene are never identical.

Try this first
Set noise to 0. The blur is now slightly worse: it
      smears the figure's outline, so it flags a few pixels just outside it. Now raise the
      noise to 15 without touching anything else. The left lane fills with confetti and the
      right one barely moves.

    
The tempting wrong fix
Leave the noise high and try to fix the left lane by
      raising the threshold instead. You can suppress the confetti, but by the time it is gone
      the figure has gone with it. Blur removes the noise; threshold only hides it.

    
Why the minimum of two
Switch to one diff only. A single subtraction
      flags where the figure is and where it just was, so you get two ghosts. Taking the
      minimum of the before and after differences keeps only the overlap, which is the figure
      now.

    
The connection back
The blur is the Gaussian from the filtering half of this
      lecture, doing a job that has nothing to do with making pictures look nice. It is there
      because averaging shrinks independent noise and leaves real structure alone.
