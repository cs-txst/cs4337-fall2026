Shadow on the Page

The demo that justifies adaptive thresholding. Crank the shadow across a page of text and watch one global cut fail everywhere at once, no matter where you put it.

The lighting

A phone camera over a book. The ink is the same darkness everywhere; only the light falling on the paper changes.

Adaptive cut

Each pixel gets its own T: the average of the block around it, minus C. C is what stops flat paper from turning into noise.

This is the whole argument. The ink dips are the same depth everywhere, but they sit on a sloping floor. A horizontal line cannot stay between the ink and the paper for the whole row. A line that bends can.

Try this first
Leave the shadow at 0 and ask the class which method they
      would ship. Both work. Then push the shadow to 60 and ask again, before revealing the
      middle panel.

    
The trap in blockSize
Drag it down to 5. The paper itself starts thresholding
      into noise, because a tiny block sees only paper and has no contrast to work with.
      Drag it to 61 and adaptive slowly turns back into a global threshold.

    
The trap in C
Set C to 0 on a clean page. Half the blank paper turns black.
      C is the margin that says only call it ink if it is meaningfully darker than its
      neighbours.

    
Where this ships
Every phone document scanner runs something like this.
      It is why a photo of a page in bad light still comes out as clean black text, and it
      is deck slide 22.
