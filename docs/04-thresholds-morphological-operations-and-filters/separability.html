Two Passes, One Blur

A big Gaussian blur is really two small ones. The intermediate image, which never appears on a slide, is the whole idea, and it is sitting in the middle of this page.

The kernel

0 means let OpenCV derive it from the size.

The arithmetic

one 2D pass 225 multiplies per pixel, k x k
two 1D passes 30 multiplies per pixel, k + k

Speedup 7.5x, and it grows with the kernel. At 31 x 31 it is over fifteen times less arithmetic for a bit-identical result.

Is it really identical?

Try this first
Switch to Horizontal only and ask the class what is
      missing. The picture is smeared sideways and still sharp vertically. That half-finished
      image is what a slide can never show you.

    
Then push the size
Drag the kernel to 31 x 31. The 2D count runs away to
      961 while the separable count crawls to 62. The gap is the whole reason
      GaussianBlur stays usable at large radii.

    
The catch
Not every kernel separates. A Gaussian does, a box does, and
      Sobel does. An arbitrary 3 x 3 of hand-picked numbers almost certainly does not, and
      then you are stuck paying k squared.

    
The check
The max difference stays at rounding level, not zero, because
      the two orders of operations accumulate floating point error differently. Anything under
      about one gray level means the results are the same picture.
