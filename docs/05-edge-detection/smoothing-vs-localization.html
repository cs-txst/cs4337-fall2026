Start at zero
With no blur the derivative is a forest. The true step is in
      there, and so are dozens of noise peaks just as tall. This is slide 13's question, and
      the answer is that you cannot tell.

    
Then climb slowly
The false peaks fall away fast: 16 of them at sigma 0,
      7 at sigma 0.75, and none by sigma 1.25. That is the whole argument for smoothing
      first, and so far it has cost almost nothing.

    
Keep going
Now watch the third card instead of the picture. The bar's contrast
      drains away as the blur fills it in, and once it is shallower than the sensor noise it is
      gone for good: on the settings this page opens with, that happens at
      sigma 5. The edges did not move out of position. They were erased.

    
The honest version of the tradeoff
Smoothing does not push an isolated edge
      out of position. It does two other things: it widens the peak, from 3 pixels at
      sigma 0 to 10 at sigma 4, so you know where the edge is less precisely, and it
      fills in anything small nearby. Slide 19's three panels are three points on this
      slider, and none of them is the right answer until you say what you are looking for.
