Measured, not guessed
At 180 you get ten lines, and nine of them share θ between 120° and 123°. They fall into two groups of ρ: four near 14 to 20, sitting on the boundary between the dark shoulder and the road, and five between 62 and 88, sitting on the several edges of the white stripe. So the vote does find both real linear features, and it finds each of them four or five times over, once per sub-edge. The tenth line, at θ = 135°, follows neither and cuts across both.

    
No setting returns the stripe once
Drop the threshold to 128 and 134 lines bury the picture; raise it to 250 and 2 survive; at 300 there are none. Collapsing a cluster of peaks into one detection needs non-maximum suppression, not a better threshold. The strongest cell holds 290 votes, about 1 percent of the 27,823 edge pixels, because the asphalt texture contributes far more edge pixels than either feature does.

    
Where the lines come from
The image is the road photograph the deck already uses on the Voting for Lines slides, and the edge map is cv.Canny(gray, 100, 200). The line list is OpenCV's: every accumulator peak was exported once with its vote count, and the slider filters that table, which is exactly what the threshold argument does inside cv.HoughLines. Checked against cv.HoughLines at all 176 integer thresholds in this range: identical at 173, and differing by a single line at 132, 144 and 276, where two cells tie on votes and the tie breaks the other way in single precision.
