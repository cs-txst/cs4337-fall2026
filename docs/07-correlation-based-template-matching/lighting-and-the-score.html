What the centering cancels
Adding the same number to every pixel moves the patch's mean by exactly that number, so subtracting the mean undoes it. Drag the brightness on the face with clipping off: TM_CCOEFF_NORMED stays at 0.799. The cosine creeps up toward 1, because a brighter patch looks more like any other array of positive numbers.

    
Why the cosine is not enough
Dividing by the lengths cancels a contrast change in both scores, which is why both survive "in shade". But without centering every patch is all positive, and all-positive arrays point in nearly the same direction: the cosine gives the face 0.988, the trees 0.961 and the car 0.906. Normalized correlation gives 0.799, 0.127 and 0.029.

    
Where the invariance ends
A real camera stores 0 to 255. Push the gain or the offset far enough and pixels clip, which is not a gain or an offset any more, and the score finally drops. Every score here is what cv.matchTemplate returns for this patch and template; the page's arithmetic was checked against it at eighteen settings.
