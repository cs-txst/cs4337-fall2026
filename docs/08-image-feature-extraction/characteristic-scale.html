Why the normalization is not optional
A Gaussian of width sigma spreads a fixed amount of weight over an area that grows as sigma squared, so its response to any fixed structure shrinks at the same rate. For a single ideal blob that makes the unnormalized response fall away monotonically and the maximum sit at the smallest sigma tried. On a photograph, which has structure at every scale, what you see instead is a bias: switch the factor off here and both peaks slide toward the small end and the ratio between the two copies stops matching the size ratio. Either way the peak has stopped being a property of the scene.

    
Difference of Gaussians
What is plotted is the difference of two Gaussian blurs at nearby widths, which is what SIFT uses. It approximates the Laplacian closely enough that the peak lands in the same place, and it costs almost nothing extra because the blurred copies have to be built anyway in order to step through scales.

    
From sigma to a radius
A circular blob of radius r gives its strongest normalized Laplacian response at sigma equal to r over the square root of 2. That is why the circles drawn by the detector have radius sigma times the square root of 2, and it is how a peak in sigma becomes a patch size for the descriptor to work on.
