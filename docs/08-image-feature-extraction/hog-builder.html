Why 180 degrees and not 360
Switch the convention to signed and watch the histogram split into two halves that mirror each other. An edge and the same edge with light and dark swapped point in opposite directions, and a pedestrian detector should not care which side of a person happens to be brighter than the wall behind them. Folding the circle in half throws away that distinction on purpose. It is also cheaper: 9 bins cover 180 degrees at 20 degrees each, and covering the full circle at that same resolution would take 18 bins and twice the descriptor.

    
Why blocks overlap
Set the stride to one cell and the counter says most cells are used four times. Each of those four copies is normalized against a different set of neighbors, so a cell next to a bright region is represented both with and without that region's influence. Push the stride up to match the block size and the overlap disappears, the descriptor gets much shorter, and the detector gets noticeably worse.

    
324 and 3,780
The lecture's worked example counts 36 numbers per block times 9 overlapping blocks, which is 324. OpenCV's default person window gives 7 by 15 blocks, so 105 times 36 is 3,780. The formula never changed; the window did. Set cellSize to 8, blockSize to 2 and blockStride to 1 here and the readout lands on 3,780, the same number hog.compute returns in the notebook.
