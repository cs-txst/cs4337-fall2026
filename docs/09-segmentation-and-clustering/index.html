Segmentation and Clustering Demos

Five interactive pages for Segmentation and Clustering, one for each choice the lecture says you have to make: K and the starting centers, how much position counts, the mean shift window, the watershed markers, and OpenCV's two radii. Each page opens on its own, runs offline, and prints the call behind every control.

Every method needs a choice

No segmentation method here runs without a knob, and the lecture's point is that the knob decides the answer. Each page puts one of them on a slider and shows the photo where it goes wrong.

They run offline

No network, no installs, no libraries. The photo, the distances and the precomputed OpenCV results sit inside each file, so a copied folder or a memory stick behaves exactly the same on a classroom projector.

Measured, not asserted

The K-means pages reach the same centers and totals as scikit-learn from the same start, the mean shift page finds the same number of peaks as scikit-learn at every bandwidth, and the watershed page finds the same regions as cv.watershed.

Add each pixel's (x, y) to its color and scale it with one slider, from ignored to raw pixel coordinates. The segments go from scattered, to compact, to five blocks that follow nothing in the photo.

The Window That Climbs

1,200 pixels by their u* and v*. Drop a window and step it to the mean of what it covers until it stops on a peak. The bandwidth alone decides whether there are 51 peaks, 5, or 1.

Where the images come from

The tennis court is tennis_court2.jpg from the notebook's data/ folder, and the disks are drawn by the same three lines the notebook uses, so anything seen here can be reproduced by running one cell.

What these are for

Slides show one result per method. The gap a deck leaves is what happens as the choice changes, and that is the only thing these pages do.

Reading the code bar

Every page ends with the calls its controls stand for, with the values the controls are currently on highlighted. Copy it into the notebook and the same thing happens there.
