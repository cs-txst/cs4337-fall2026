K-Means One Step at a Time

Every pixel of the tennis court photo is a point in color space. Press Assign and each pixel joins its nearest center; press Update and each center moves to the mean of its pixels. The total squared distance can only go down, and where it stops depends on where it started.

Why it always stops
Assign gives every pixel the center that is closest to it, which cannot increase the total. Update moves every center to the mean of its pixels, and the mean is exactly the point with the smallest total squared distance to them, so that cannot increase it either. A total that never goes up and has finitely many possible assignments has to stop changing.

    
Why it stops in different places
Stopping only means no single step helps. At K = 5 the 20 start numbers end at four different totals: 32.9 million (random centers 9 times out of 20, K-means++ 11 times), 38.3, 38.4 and 44.6 million. That is why cv.kmeans takes an attempts argument and keeps the best of several starts. In the best one, both green courts share a cluster with the gray asphalt, exactly as on the full-size photo.

    
What runs here
The page runs Lloyd's algorithm on every fourth pixel of the photo in each direction (256 by 192 real pixel values, not averages), which is the algorithm cv.kmeans runs. Given the same starting centers it reaches the same centers and the same total as scikit-learn's KMeans. Random centers are drawn uniformly inside the range of the data, as cv.KMEANS_RANDOM_CENTERS does; an empty cluster is restarted on the pixel farthest from its center.
