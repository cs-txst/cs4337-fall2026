Height is probability per unit
A density's height tells you how much probability sits near a value per unit of r. Multiply by a small width and you get a probability: the last two readouts agree whenever the interval is narrow. Because r only ranges over a fraction of a unit, the height has to be large for the area to reach 1.

    
Where 391 comes from
The rg model multiplies two such curves. With the hand's standard deviations, 0.030 for r and 0.013 for g, the peaks are 13.1 and 29.8, and their product is 391. The best pixel of frame20 scores 390.7. The RGB model's scores are tiny for the opposite reason: standard deviations near 40 make each curve wide and low.

    
What a histogram stores
A normalized histogram stores the area of each bin, not a height, so every value is a real probability and they sum to 1. That is why the histogram scores never exceed 1 and the density scores can.
