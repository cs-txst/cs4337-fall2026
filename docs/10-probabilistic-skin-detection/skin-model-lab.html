Why the thresholds look so different
The Gaussian scores are densities, so their scale depends on how narrow the model is: about 0.0000009 at most for R, G, B, about 391 for r, g. The histogram score is a probability per bin, at most 0.0027. Only the Bayes score is a probability of skin, between 0 and 1. A threshold means nothing until you know which of these it is applied to.

    
What the rectangle shows
A Gaussian learns whatever it is shown. Train it on the wall and it finds wall, with the same confidence it had for skin. The histograms here were trained on large labeled collections and ship with the notebook as data/positive_histogram.npy and data/negative_histogram.npy, so the rectangle does not affect them.

    
What runs here
The notebook's own formulas, on the notebook's two frames and histograms. With the hand rectangle and the notebook's thresholds, the share of frame20 kept matches NumPy: 23.0% for R, G, B, 9.5% for r, g, 9.4% for the skin histogram and 8.8% for Bayes.
