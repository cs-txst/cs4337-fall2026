Spin the Line

The lecture asks which line through the data keeps the distances between points best, and then answers with an eigenvector of the covariance matrix. Spin a line through the 100 points of the deck's example and see that these are the same question: the angle that keeps the most variance, the angle that loses the least distance, and the first eigenvector all coincide.

What it shows
Every point splits into two parts at a right angle: its shadow on the line and the perpendicular part the line throws away. By Pythagoras the two squared lengths add up to the squared distance from the average, so the variance kept plus the variance lost is always the total variance, the trace of the covariance matrix. Keeping more means losing less, and the angle that keeps the most is the first eigenvector.

    
Distances between points
Shadows can only be closer together than the points themselves. Add up, over all 4,950 pairs, how much each squared distance shrinks: the total is exactly n(n - 1) times the variance lost, for every line through the average and every cloud. So the line that keeps the most variance is also the line that keeps pairwise distances best.

    
Where the data come from
The 100 points are the deck's 2D example, generated with np.random.default_rng(11) along a 30° direction and stored with 4 decimals. The round cloud is 10 points copied at 10 rotations of 36°, so its covariance is the same in every direction. Variances divide by n - 1, like np.cov.
