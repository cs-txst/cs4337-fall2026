Templates, not averages
Each step adds a little of every photo of a person to that person's row and subtracts a little of the other people's photos, weighted by how wrong the probabilities still are. From W = 0 every probability is 1/10, so the very first step makes each row exactly proportional to that person's average face minus the average of all faces: the shared features cancel and what sets the person apart remains. Later steps make the rows larger and add corrections where people are still confused; "new since step 1" shows those corrections, about a third of each row by step 500.

    
What it costs and what it gets
After 500 steps at learning rate 0.001 the classifier gets all 50 training photos and 9 of the 10 new ones right, the same as nearest neighbor on normalized pixels and one more than the average faces. It stores 30,010 numbers instead of 50 photos of 3,000.

    
Too slow, too fast
At 0.0001 the loss is still 1.96 after 50 steps; at 0.01 it is down to 0.34 after 10. How large is too large depends on the loss: the spiral network trains well at 3 and dies at 10.
