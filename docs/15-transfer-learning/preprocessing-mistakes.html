Preprocessing Mistakes

The notebook's fine-tuned ResNet-18 was trained on photos prepared exactly as weights.transforms() says: RGB order, resized and center-cropped to 224 × 224, normalized with the ImageNet mean and standard deviation. Get one step wrong and see what it costs, on one photo and on all 340 test photos. Every prediction here was computed by the network itself, in PyTorch.

BGR swaps red and blue
cv.imread returns blue, green, red. A network trained on red, green, blue reads the blue channel as red: bluebells turn red and become tiger lilies, tiger lilies turn blue and become bluebells. Nothing crashes; the answers are just wrong.

    
Normalization changes every value
The first layer learned weights for inputs centered near zero, roughly −2 to +2.6. Values from 0 to 1 look the same on screen, but every first-layer response shifts.

    
Stretching is cheap here, not in general
Most photos in this dataset are close to square, so squashing them to 224 × 224 barely changes their shape and costs about a point. A 4:3 or 16:9 phone photo is distorted far more.
