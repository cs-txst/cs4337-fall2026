How Coarse Can the Decision Be?

ResNet-18 turns a 256 × 256 photo into an 8 × 8 grid of features. Suppose a network knew the correct answer perfectly, but only on a coarse grid: for every cell, the fraction of its pixels that are person. This page enlarges that grid back to full size and scores it against the hand-drawn mask. The result is the best IoU any network can reach if it decides at that resolution.

The coarse network is at its ceiling
Choose 8 cells and bilinear. The best possible IoU over the test photos is about 0.54, and the notebook's coarse network measures 0.54. It is not badly trained. Sixty-four decisions cannot describe the outline of a person, whatever the network knows.

    
Resolution buys IoU
Each doubling of the grid raises the ceiling. The decoder exists to make the decision at a finer grid, and the skip connections give it the information it needs to make that finer decision well.

    
Thin parts go first
Look at what is blue at 8 and 16 cells: legs, arms and heads. A cell that is less than half person is called background, so parts thinner than a cell vanish. Small and distant people disappear entirely.
