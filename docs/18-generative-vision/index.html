Generative Vision: Demos

Four interactive pages for networks that make images: the latent space of an autoencoder, a GAN's training run, and diffusion, first as points in the plane and then on the digits of the lecture's notebook. Each page opens on its own, runs offline, and prints the Python code behind its controls.

The notebook's own networks

The autoencoder, the GAN and the diffusion model are the ones the lecture's notebook trains on handwritten digits. The pages show their weights, their training history and their recorded outputs, not illustrations.

They run offline

No network, no installs, no libraries. The autoencoder's decoder and the two-dimensional diffusion run live in the browser. The GAN's and the diffusion model's images were recorded in the notebook.

Checked against the notebook

The decoder in the page reproduces PyTorch's output to 10−8, the two-dimensional denoiser and its generation step match a NumPy reference to 10−7, and the recorded images and counts are the notebook's own arrays.

Diffusion, Step by Step

Recordings of the notebook's diffusion model: the image, the predicted noise and the estimate of the clean digit at every recorded moment, for ten digits, two starting noises and four step counts.

slides 54 to 60 digitstepsscrub
TRY: with 100 steps, find the first moment at which you can read the digit in the estimate. How noisy is the image itself at that moment?

Where the data come from

Lectures/18 - Generative Vision/code/make-viz-data.py packs the autoencoder's decoder, the GAN's recorded training run and the diffusion model's recorded generations, all saved by the notebook, into _build/data-18.json, with NumPy's answers for checking the pages.

What these are for

Planning and self-study: open a page while preparing the slides it serves, or point students to it after class. Each opens on its own; nothing here needs the others.

Reading the code bar

The code bars repeat the notebook's own lines: add_noise and generate for diffusion, the two-part training step of the GAN, and the autoencoder's decode.
