From far away, aim for the middle
At a high noise level, switch the arrows on: they all point to the center of the spiral. A very noisy point could have come from any data point, so the best single estimate is the average of all of them. This is the blurry "average digit" of the notebook's first generation steps.

    
Up close, aim for the nearest arm
At a low noise level each arrow points to the nearest part of the spiral. The decision of where to land is made gradually: first the rough region, then the arm, then the exact place.

    
Too few steps lose the variety
Half of the data lies on the outer half of the spiral, and with 100 or 1,000 steps half of the generated points land there. With 20 steps it is about 43%, and with 5 steps about 4%: big steps follow the early "aim for the middle" estimates too far, and nearly everything ends on the inner turns.
